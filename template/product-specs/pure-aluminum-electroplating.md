# AlumiPlate — Pure Aluminum Electroplating

## What it is

AlumiPlate electrodeposits a layer of aluminum, typically more than 99.99% pure, onto a part. Water-based chemistry can't deposit aluminum, so the process uses an enclosed, automated, solvent-based bath. The result is a dense, pore-free, conformal aluminum layer that protects the substrate and can be anodized.

## Who it is for

- **Engineers** with an unsolved problem: corrosion, high temperature, hydrogen embrittlement, plasma exposure, or galvanic issues. This is the primary audience.
- **Specifiers** (VPs, PhDs, design authorities) who set coating requirements across a product line. This is the highest-value audience.
- **Buyers** searching an exact spec (MIL-DTL-83488, a cadmium replacement). Capture these, but they're lower value.

## Substrates

Aluminum alloys, high-strength and stainless steels, copper and copper alloys, iron, nickel alloys and superalloys, carbon and metal-matrix composites. Some polymers, plastics, and ceramics can be plated after metallization.

## Limits and non-goals

- **Part size:** about 30 inches maximum length; tanks are about 20" × 30" × 23". Slightly larger parts may be possible depending on geometry, after drawing review.
- **Cost:** a premium process with tooling and masking costs. It doesn't compete on price; IVD is cheaper outside semiconductor (Fathom 2026-10-02).
- **Certifications:** not ISO or NADCAP certified. This can lose transactional, spec-driven orders.
- **Not offered as a growth focus:** commodity MIL-DTL-5541 work.
