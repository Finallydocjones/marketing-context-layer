# AlumiPlate — Anodizing over Electroplated Aluminum

## What it is

AlumiPlate first plates a dense, high-purity aluminum layer onto a part, then anodizes that layer. This lets materials that can't normally be anodized get an anodized aluminum oxide surface. Examples are stainless steel, high-strength steel, copper, castings, silicon, and some plastics.

## Who it is for

Engineers who need an anodized surface for corrosion protection, electrical insulation, or plasma resistance on a non-aluminum part. Semiconductor chamber components are a key case.

## Key capabilities

| Property | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Substrates | Stainless and high-strength steels, copper, castings, plastics, silicon, other non-aluminum materials | FAQ, semiconductor page |
| Plating thickness for anodizing | 50–100% of the anodized layer thickness | FAQ |
| Dielectric | Anodized electroplated Al breakdown voltage >2,000 V per 0.001 in of oxide | Semiconductor page |
| Oxide quality | Fewer voids and impurities than alumina grown from less pure alloys | Semiconductor page |
| Corrosion | Anodized pure Al outperforms anodized aluminum alloys in HCl bubble testing (qualitative; no figures published) | Semiconductor page |

## Limits and non-goals

- Same part-size and cost limits as the core plating service.
- **This is not commodity MIL-DTL-5541 conversion-coating work**, and it must not be marketed that way. See policy.
