# Hexavalent Chromium Restrictions

Hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)) has long been used in chromate conversion coatings over aluminum, including the Type II treatment under MIL-DTL-83488. It's a carcinogen, and its use is being restricted.

## U.S. defense: DFARS 252.223-7008

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Clause | DFARS 252.223-7008, Prohibition of Hexavalent Chromium (JAN 2023) |
| What it prohibits | Delivering supplies or construction materials containing Cr(VI) above **0.1% by weight in any homogeneous material**, or that require removing or reapplying Cr(VI) during sustainment |
| Exceptions | By-products of manufacturing; otherwise only with Contracting Officer authorization |
| Flowdown | Must be included in all subcontracts, including commercial |
| Source | acquisition.gov |

## EU: REACH

- Chromium trioxide and other Cr(VI) compounds have required REACH **authorization** for specific uses.
- In 2025, ECHA proposed replacing this with a broad **Annex XVII restriction** on 13 Cr(VI) substances at ≥0.01% by weight. Electroplating and other surface treatment could continue only under strict exposure limits, with an 18-month transition. A six-month public consultation began in June 2025; RAC/SEAC opinions and a Commission decision follow.
- **Status is moving. Re-check ECHA before citing.**

## AlumiPlate's relationship

- Electroplated aluminum can be followed by **trivalent chromate** or **non-chromate** treatments, reducing or eliminating hex-chrome requirements (Fathom 2026-09-23; alumiplate.com 83488 page).
- Gus noted hex chrome use is increasingly limited, mainly to military customers.

## How to talk about it

- ✅ "Compatible with trivalent and non-chromate post-treatments, helping programs reduce hexavalent chromium"
- ❌ Don't say AlumiPlate makes a customer "DFARS compliant". Compliance depends on the whole deliverable and contract.
- ❌ Don't state the EU restriction as final until it's adopted.
