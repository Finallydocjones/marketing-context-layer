# AlumiPlate — Buyer and Engineer FAQ

These answers are paraphrased from the site's FAQ and tightened. Each has one canonical answer. Once approved, use them for FAQ pages, FAQ schema, and AI answer content.

### What is a replacement for cadmium plating?
AlumiPlate's electroplated pure aluminum is a nontoxic, drop-in replacement for cadmium plating. It complies with RoHS, REACH, and WEEE. AlumiPlate reports that it outperforms cadmium in salt-fog testing. *Source: FAQ*

### How pure is AlumiPlate's aluminum coating?
It's typically more than 99.99% pure aluminum. Baths are tightly controlled and filtered, and purity is verified by Glow Discharge Mass Spectrometry (GDMS). *Source: FAQ*

### How thick can the aluminum plating be?
Corrosion-protection coatings are typically 10–25 µm. Most applications fall between 10 and 100+ µm. Deposits up to about 500 µm are possible for machinable uses such as metal mirrors. For anodizing, the plating is 50–100% of the anodized layer thickness. *Source: FAQ*

### How does it hold up in salt fog?
AlumiPlate reports that a 10 µm layer exceeds 1,000 hours of ASTM B117 salt fog and survives 336+ hours of ASTM G85 SO2 testing. *Source: FAQ*

### Can you plate MIL-DTL-83488 parts or replace IVD aluminum?
Yes. Both IVD and electroplated aluminum meet MIL-DTL-83488, and AlumiPlate can certify compliance. A thinner class of electroplated aluminum can often replace a thicker IVD coating, with better dimensional control and no line-of-sight limitation. *Source: FAQ*

### Can you anodize stainless steel or other non-aluminum parts?
Yes. AlumiPlate plates a dense, pure aluminum layer and then anodizes it. That works on stainless and high-strength steels, copper, castings, and some plastics. *Source: FAQ*

### What materials can be plated?
Most engineered metals: many steels, aluminum alloys, stainless and high-temperature steels, copper alloys, nickel alloys, and some composites. Some plastics and ceramics can be plated after metallization. *Source: FAQ*

### What's the largest part you can plate?
Tanks fit parts up to about 20" × 30" × 23", or roughly 30 inches long. Slightly larger parts may be possible depending on geometry. AlumiPlate reviews drawings to confirm. *Source: FAQ; Fathom 2026-10-02*

### How much does it cost and how long does it take?
Cost depends on coating thickness, masking, and tooling. AlumiPlate gives a firm quote after reviewing drawings. Most jobs finish in 2–3 weeks, with expedite options. *Source: FAQ*

### Can I get test samples?
Yes. AlumiPlate offers free 3" × 6" coated coupons in 4130 steel, C110 copper, 6061 aluminum, and 316 stainless steel. Custom samples usually carry a small charge. *Source: FAQ*

### Is AlumiPlate ISO or NADCAP certified?
Not currently. AlumiPlate's quality systems are based on ISO 9001. ### How does aluminum electroplating work?
Aluminum can't be plated from water-based solutions, because hydrogen would evolve instead. AlumiPlate uses a solvent-based chemistry in enclosed, automated, continuously monitored baths. *Source: FAQ*

### Is the process safe?
The process is fully enclosed, automated, and continuously monitored, with a 20+ year safety record. With recycling and distillation, it has the potential for zero emissions and zero hazardous waste. *Source: FAQ*

### Do you license the process?
Yes. Plating services are offered from AlumiPlate's Minnesota facility, and the company is open to licensing and IP transfer for customers who need the capability at their own site. Inquiries go to info@alumiplate.com. *Source: FAQ*
