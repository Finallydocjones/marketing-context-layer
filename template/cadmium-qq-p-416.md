# Cadmium Plating (AMS-QQ-P-416) and Cadmium Restrictions

## What it is

**AMS-QQ-P-416** is the SAE Aerospace Material Specification for electrodeposited cadmium plating. It took over from the legacy federal spec **QQ-P-416** (last revision F). Buyers and engineers still search the old "QQ-P-416" designation.

| Item | Value | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Current revision | **AMS-QQ-P-416G**, revised 8 September 2022 | 2026-10-14 |
| Issuer | SAE International | — |
| Official source | saemobilus.sae.org (copyrighted; link, don't copy) | — |

## Why it matters to AlumiPlate

Cadmium is toxic and increasingly restricted, so engineers searching QQ-P-416 are often looking for an **alternative**. AlumiPlate positions pure electroplated aluminum as a **nontoxic, drop-in cadmium replacement**. Gus named QQ-P-416 as a spec buyers search (Fathom 2026-10-02).

## Restrictions driving the switch

- **EU RoHS (2011/65/EU):** cadmium is limited to 0.01% by weight in homogeneous materials of covered electrical/electronic equipment.
- **EU REACH Annex XVII:** cadmium restrictions include limits on cadmium plating in many sectors, with exemptions (e.g., certain aerospace and defense uses). *Confirm the current entry wording on ECHA before citing specifics.*
- **U.S. DoD:** the AlumiPlate FAQ says the DoD has identified the coating as a preferred cadmium replacement. Source document still needed (OPEN-QUESTIONS #5).

## How to talk about it

- ✅ "A nontoxic, RoHS- and REACH-compliant alternative to cadmium plating"
- ✅ "AlumiPlate reports its coating outperforms cadmium in ASTM B117 salt-fog testing"
- ❌ Don't say AlumiPlate "meets AMS-QQ-P-416". That's a cadmium spec; aluminum replaces it, it doesn't comply with it.
- ❌ Don't cite DoD preference until the source is confirmed.
