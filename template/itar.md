# ITAR — International Traffic in Arms Regulations

## What it is

U.S. regulations (22 CFR Parts 120–130), administered by the State Department's **Directorate of Defense Trade Controls (DDTC)**. They control the export of defense articles, defense services, and related **technical data** on the U.S. Munitions List. U.S. manufacturers of defense articles or furnishers of defense services generally must register with DDTC (22 CFR Part 122).

## AlumiPlate's relationship

- **ITAR registered** with DDTC (alumiplate.com approvals page; ITAR seal in site footer).
- That's a **trust signal** for defense buyers. Registration is not a "certification". Say "ITAR registered".

## Why this matters for marketing (read this)

ITAR controls **technical data**, not just hardware. Process details, part drawings, specs, and test data tied to defense programs can be export-controlled. "Export" includes sharing with foreign persons inside the U.S. and posting publicly.

**Rules for Rocket55 until AlumiPlate confirms what's controlled (OPEN-QUESTIONS #14):**

1. **Never put customer drawings, part numbers, program names, or customer test data** in this repo, in Profound, in AI tools, or in published content.
2. Use only technical information AlumiPlate has **already published** on alumiplate.com, or has explicitly cleared for public release.
3. Have AlumiPlate review **gated technical resources** (white papers, characterization reports) for export control before posting, even behind a form.
4. Name defense programs only if AlumiPlate already names them publicly (e.g., its MIL-DTL-83488 Programs page). Confirm first.
5. Third-party AI tools may process data on servers or with staff outside the U.S. Treat that as a reason to keep controlled data out of every AI tool.

## How to talk about it

- ✅ "ITAR registered"
- ❌ "ITAR certified" or "ITAR compliant products" (vague, and not accurate wording)
