# MIL-DTL-83488 — Coating, Aluminum, High Purity

## What it is

A U.S. Department of Defense detail specification for coating parts with **high-purity aluminum (99%+)**. It covers low-alloy steel, stainless steel, aluminum alloy, copper alloy, and titanium alloy parts. It was formerly designated MIL-C-83488.

| Item | Value | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Latest revision found | **MIL-DTL-83488D**, dated 1 April 1999 (superseded MIL-C-83488C) | 2026-10-14 |
| Issuer | U.S. Department of Defense | — |
| Official source | ASSIST (quicksearch.dla.mil). Confirm the current revision and any notices there before citing. | — |

Both **IVD (ion vapor deposited) aluminum** and **electrodeposited aluminum** can meet this spec. Per AlumiPlate's page, the spec's paragraph 6.1 recognizes electrodeposition as a compliant process.

## Requirements (as summarized on AlumiPlate's spec page)

**Classes** set coating thickness and the expected salt-fog resistance:

| Class | Minimum thickness |
|---|---|
| Class 1 | 0.001 in (≈25 µm) |
| Class 2 | 0.0005 in (≈13 µm) |
| Class 3 | 0.0003 in (≈8 µm) |

**Types** describe the supplementary treatment:

- **Type I:** as-coated, no supplementary treatment
- **Type II:** supplementary chromate treatment (per MIL-DTL-5541). Trivalent and non-chromate options are available for RoHS/REACH needs.

**Other requirements:**

- Purity >99.0% Al
- Adhesion (no flaking, peeling, or blistering)
- Corrosion resistance in ASTM B117 salt spray, ranging from 168 to 672 hours depending on class

*Confirm the class and type definitions against the spec text on ASSIST before publishing them as spec facts. They're currently sourced from AlumiPlate's own page.*

## AlumiPlate's relationship

- **Complies** with MIL-DTL-83488 and **can certify compliance** per order. Use this wording. Don't say "certified to" (OPEN-QUESTIONS #2).
- Stated advantages over the spec minimums: typically >99.99% purity vs. the >99.0% required, and AlumiPlate reports at least twice the required salt-fog hours. The site's figure is "366+ to 1344+ hours" vs. a 168–672 h requirement; 366 looks like a typo for 336 (OPEN-QUESTIONS #12).
- Advantages over IVD for this spec: no line-of-sight limit, and a thinner class can often replace thicker IVD with better dimensional control.

## How to talk about it

- This is the **primary spec for aerospace/defense buyers**, and a priority keyword and page.
- **It doesn't apply to semiconductor or quantum work.** Never use it as a proof point there.
- People searching this spec are often transactional buyers (1-star leads). Use the page to lead them toward engineering support and problem-solving content.
